# Secpar writing

Read this before changing app labels, website text or help pages.

Write the action or answer first. Use the words shown in the app. Name the device,
setting and symptom. Explain one useful step at a time. End when the reader has
what they need. Use headings and lists when they make instructions easier to scan.

Remove promotional adjectives, empty reassurances, grand claims, canned openings,
repeated summaries and unnecessary descriptions of obvious controls. Avoid
"not X, but Y", "not just X", forced groups of three, rhetorical questions,
excessive em dashes, faux-friendly praise and strings of abstract nouns. Prefer
"use", "is", "shows", "open" and "turn on" over inflated substitutes. Never invent
measurements or describe an unfinished feature as available.

These are editing rules, not a test of who wrote a sentence. Keep useful technical
terms when the reader needs them. Write original Secpar instructions; research
another product's behavior without copying its support articles.

## Patterns to keep out (researched 2026-11-07)

Vocabulary: "delve", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "streamline", "harness", "tapestry",
"landscape", "ecosystem", "pivotal", "crucial", "game-changing". Adverbs that inflate
("quietly", "deeply", "fundamentally"). "Serves as", "stands as", "marks" instead of "is".

Sentences: "It's not X, it's Y" and "Not X. Not Y. Just Z." A question answered at once
("The result? ..."). Three items every time. The same opening repeated. "Importantly",
"Notably", "It's worth noting". Trailing "-ing" clauses that add nothing ("reflecting a
broader shift"). "From X to Y" with nothing between them.

Paragraphs and tone: one-line fragments for emphasis. "Here's the kicker". "Think of it
as". "Imagine a world where". "Let's break this down". "The truth is simple". "Experts
say". Invented names for ordinary things. Stakes inflated past the subject. "Despite
these challenges". Compliment before the correction.

Formatting: em dashes (use none; a comma or full stop does the job). Every bullet opening
with a bold label. Arrows and curly decoration where plain text works. A summary after
every section, an announced conclusion ("In conclusion"), one point said five ways.

Check: could the sentence be said by anyone about any product? Cut it. Say what the thing
does, where to tap, what happens next.
